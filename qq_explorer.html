What is a Q-Q plot?

Many analytics models assume your data follows a particular distribution, for example “errors are normal” or “customers arrive at random”. A Q-Q plot (quantile-quantile plot) is a quick picture that answers: does my data have the shape I'm assuming?

It lines up your data's percentiles with the percentiles that the assumed distribution would have. If the shapes match, the points form a straight line. If they don't, the way the line bends tells you how they differ.

Quantile = percentile

The 10% quantile is the value that 10% of the data falls below; the 50% quantile is the median. “Q-Q” means we plot quantiles of one thing against quantiles of another.

How it's built: a 5-number example

  1. Sort the data. Five exam scores: 61, 70, 74, 79, 92.
  2. Give each value a percentile. With 5 values, each one sits in the middle of its fifth of the data: 10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90%.
  3. Look up the same percentiles of the reference distribution. For a standard normal: −1.28, −0.52, 0, 0.52, 1.28.
  4. Plot each pair (reference percentile, data value) and see whether the points form a straight line.

Why a straight line means “same shape”

If your data is just the reference distribution stretched (a different spread) and slid (a different center), every percentile is stretched and slid the same way, so the pairs fall on a straight line. Bends appear only where the shape differs, usually in the tails.

Reading a Q-Q plot

A Q-Q plot sorts the data and plots each value against the matching quantile of a reference distribution (the 1st percentile against the 1st percentile, the median against the median, and so on). If the data come from that distribution, the points fall along a straight line.

Three questions to ask

1. Is it straight? Straight means the shapes match. 2. Where does it bend? The ends show the tails. 3. Slope and position of a straight line compare spread and center.

Common traps

Small samples wobble even when the distribution is right, so judge the overall pattern, not a few points (try sample size 25 in Explore). Steps or flat runs mean discrete data (whole-number counts), not a bad fit. A Q-Q plot only compares with the reference you chose: “not normal” doesn't tell you which distribution it is, so compare with a better candidate next.
