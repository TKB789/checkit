Module 11: Variable Selection

Understanding trade-offs and regularization techniques to build robust models.

The Bias-Variance Tradeoff

When building predictive models, the total error can be decomposed into three parts: Bias, Variance, and Irreducible Error.

High Bias (Underfitting)

The model is too simple and fails to capture the underlying patterns in the data. Assumes a rigid structure (e.g., linear) when the reality is complex.

High Variance (Overfitting)

The model is too complex and captures random noise along with the true signal. It performs perfectly on training data but poorly on unseen data.

Goal: Find the sweet spot. As model complexity increases, bias decreases but variance increases. Regularization helps us control this complexity.

Regularization: LASSO vs. Ridge

Regularization adds a penalty to the objective function, constraining the model's coefficients to prevent overfitting.

LASSO Regression (L1 Penalty)

Adds the absolute value of magnitude of coefficients as penalty term to the loss function.

Penalty = λ * Σ|βj|
  • Can shrink coefficients exactly to zero.
  • Acts as an automatic feature selection method.
  • Good when you suspect many variables are irrelevant.

Ridge Regression (L2 Penalty)

Adds the squared magnitude of coefficients as penalty term to the loss function.

Penalty = λ * Σ(βj²)
  • Shrinks coefficients towards zero, but rarely exactly zero.
  • Excellent at handling multicollinearity.
  • Keeps all features but reduces the impact of less important ones.

Module 12: Design of Experiments

Methods for testing hypotheses, comparing options, and optimizing decisions in the real world.

A/B Testing

Comparing two versions (A and B) of a single variable to determine which performs better.

  • Randomized assignment is crucial.
  • Tests one variable at a time.
  • Common in web design, marketing, and medicine.

Factorial Design

Testing multiple variables (factors) simultaneously to observe their individual and joint effects.

  • Highly efficient use of data.
  • Identifies interaction effects between variables.
  • Ex: Testing Price AND Color at the same time.

Multi-Armed Bandits

Dynamic resource allocation balancing the need to find the best option and capitalize on known good options.

  • Solves the Exploration vs. Exploitation dilemma.
  • Updates dynamically as data arrives.
  • Reduces opportunity cost compared to static A/B testing.

Module 13: Probability & Simulation

Deep dive into Quantile-Quantile (Q-Q) Plots and understanding distributions.

Interactive Q-Q Plot Visualizer

A Q-Q plot compares the quantiles of your sample data against the quantiles of a theoretical distribution (usually Normal). If the sample matches the theoretical distribution, the points will form a roughly straight 45-degree line.
